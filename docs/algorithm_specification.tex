\section{ProFair Market Observability Algorithm}
\label{sec:profair-algorithm}

\subsection{Purpose}

The ProFair Market Observability Algorithm is a transparent computational
procedure for measuring how trade fairs render markets observable through
organisations and person--organisation--fair relations. Its primary output is
a multidimensional profile rather than a single ranking.

Let $f$ denote a fair edition, $o$ an organisation proxy, and $i$ a validated
person--organisation--fair relation. The classified person-level population is

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{f}
=
\left\{
i:
G_i \in \{\mathrm{Woman},\mathrm{Man}\}
\right\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Smoothed demographic presence}

Let $W_f$ and $M_f$ be the numbers of classified women and men. To avoid
undefined ratios in small samples, the algorithm applies Jeffreys smoothing:

\begin{equation}
\widetilde{P}_{f}
=
\frac{W_f+\frac{1}{2}}
{W_f+M_f+1}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Organisational inclusion}

Let $\mathcal{O}_{f}^{C}$ be organisations containing at least one classified
person and let $\mathcal{O}_{f}^{W}$ be organisations containing at least one
classified woman:

\begin{equation}
\widetilde{I}_{f}
=
\frac{
|\mathcal{O}_{f}^{W}|+\frac{1}{2}
}{
|\mathcal{O}_{f}^{C}|+1
}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Leadership representation}

For a pre-specified leadership set $\mathcal{H}$, let $W_f^{H}$ and $M_f^{H}$
be classified women and men occupying those levels:

\begin{equation}
\widetilde{L}_{f}
=
\frac{
W_f^{H}+\frac{1}{2}
}{
W_f^{H}+M_f^{H}+1
}.
\end{equation}

The narrow definition uses H1. The broad sensitivity definition uses H1 and
H2.

\subsection{Functional-distribution similarity}

Let $\mathbf{p}_{f}^{W}$ and $\mathbf{p}_{f}^{M}$ be the distributions of
women and men over validated professional functions. Functional similarity is

\begin{equation}
F_f
=
1-
\sqrt{
\frac{
\operatorname{JSD}
\left(
\mathbf{p}_{f}^{W},
\mathbf{p}_{f}^{M}
\right)
}{
\log 2
}
},
\end{equation}

where $\operatorname{JSD}$ is the Jensen--Shannon divergence. $F_f=1$
indicates identical distributions and $F_f=0$ maximally separated
distributions.

\subsection{Area-weighted spatial representation}

Let $A_{of}$ be validated physical exhibition area and $P_{of}$ the classified
proportion of women in organisation $o$:

\begin{equation}
S_f
=
\frac{
\sum_{o \in \mathcal{O}_{f}} A_{of}P_{of}
}{
\sum_{o \in \mathcal{O}_{f}} A_{of}
}.
\end{equation}

This represents materialised spatial presence, not company value or causal
market power.

\subsection{Geographical-distribution similarity}

Let $\mathbf{g}_{f}^{W}$ and $\mathbf{g}_{f}^{M}$ be the distributions of
classified women and men over organisational geographical categories:

\begin{equation}
G_f
=
1-
\sqrt{
\frac{
\operatorname{JSD}
\left(
\mathbf{g}_{f}^{W},
\mathbf{g}_{f}^{M}
\right)
}{
\log 2
}
}.
\end{equation}

Organisational geography must not be interpreted as individual nationality or
ethnicity.

\subsection{Benchmark-adjusted representation}

For dimension $k$, let $p_{kf}$ be the observed or smoothed proportion and
$b_{kf}$ an explicit external benchmark. The signed log representation ratio is

\begin{equation}
R_{kf}
=
\log
\left(
\frac{p_{kf}}{b_{kf}}
\right).
\end{equation}

$R_{kf}<0$ indicates representation below the benchmark, $R_{kf}=0$ equality
with the benchmark, and $R_{kf}>0$ representation above the benchmark.

A bounded parity transformation is:

\begin{equation}
Q_{kf}
=
\exp
\left(
-|R_{kf}|
\right),
\qquad
0<Q_{kf}\leq 1.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Optional formative composite}

The multidimensional vector

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_{f}
=
\left(
\widetilde{P}_{f},
\widetilde{I}_{f},
\widetilde{L}_{f},
F_f,
S_f,
G_f
\right)
\end{equation}

is the primary result. A composite may be calculated only as a secondary
formative summary:

\begin{equation}
C_f
=
100
\frac{
\sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_{f}}w_kQ_{kf}
}{
\sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_{f}}w_k
},
\end{equation}

where weights $w_k$ are specified before final outcome interpretation and
$\mathcal{K}_{f}$ contains dimensions with adequate coverage and validation.

The composite does not establish that the dimensions are interchangeable or
reflect a single latent factor. Dimension-level results must always accompany
$C_f$.

\subsection{Uncertainty}

Uncertainty is estimated by resampling organisations within fair editions.
All person relations associated with a sampled organisation are retained
together. Percentile intervals are obtained from the bootstrap distribution.

Indeterminate classifications are assessed through lower and upper bounds,
and registration-intensity sensitivity is assessed through repeated
one-person-per-organisation sampling.
